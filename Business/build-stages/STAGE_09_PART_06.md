# Stage 9, Part 6 — a citation audit of the whole repo: three wrong citations found, all served to users

Stage: 9 · Part: 6 · 2026-08-09

## 0. What this part does

Part 5 made `verify_citations_live.py` discover DOIs from source instead of
a hardcoded list, taking coverage from 2 to 16. The first live run failed
one. Chasing that failure found two more of a different and worse kind.

All three lived in `domain-literature.ts` — the file that serves citations
to API users.

## 1. A DOI that does not exist

```
✗ 10.1111/j.1432-1033.1913.tb07745.x  [domain-literature.ts]
    https://api.crossref.org/works/... -> HTTP 404
```

Cited for Michaelis & Menten (1913). The `1432-1033` prefix belongs to the
*European Journal of Biochemistry*, founded 1967 — no 1913 article could
ever have carried it. It was fabricated.

The 1913 original has **no DOI at all**: *Biochemische Zeitschrift* 49,
333-369 predates DOI assignment. Replaced with Johnson & Goody's complete
English translation, which reproduces the original in full and is the
reference English-speaking authors are directed to: *Biochemistry* 50(39),
8264-8269, DOI 10.1021/bi201284u. Verified via PubMed (PMID 21888353,
PMC3381512).

## 2. A DOI that resolves but supports a different claim

Wright-Fisher's mutation rate was justified by:

```
doi: "10.1038/ng.3285"   "Variation and heritability of RECOMBINATION rate in humans"
```

Recombination rate and mutation rate are different quantities. The DOI
resolves perfectly, so the existence check passed it — **that is the whole
problem**. A citation that exists is not a citation that supports the
statement.

Corrected to Rahbari et al.'s germline-mutation paper: "Timing, rates and
spectra of human germline mutation", *Nat Genet* 48(2), 126-133,
DOI 10.1038/ng.3469, PMID 26656846, PMC4731925.

The `defaultJustification` was also corrected. It attributed the resolved
value to Rahbari, but `Tests/popgen_resolver.py` actually resolves from
stdpopsim's `HomSap mean_mutation_rate`, which carries its own bundled
citations (IHGSC 2001; Jónsson et al. 2017). The resolver was honest; the
domain-literature entry was not. `popgen_resolver.py` needed no change.

## 3. A DOI that resolves to an entirely unrelated paper

The worst of the three:

```
authors: "Tyson, J. J.", year: 1991,
title:   "Modelling the cell division cycle: cdc2 and cyclin interactions",
doi:     "10.1083/jcb.115.3.577"
```

That DOI is real and resolves. It is **"Mitotic motors"** by McIntosh &
Pfarr, *J Cell Biol* 115(3), 577-585 — a review of kinesins and the
mitotic spindle, by different authors, with no connection to Tyson's ODE
model. Verified via PubMed (PMID 1918154).

The correct DOI, already used correctly in
`Tellurium/continuous/model_building.py`, is 10.1073/pnas.88.16.7328
(PNAS 88(16), 7328-7332). So the repo simultaneously held the right DOI in
the engine and a wrong one in the user-facing citation layer.

## 4. The generalisation: existence is not correctness

Two of the three errors resolved cleanly. Any checker that only asks "does
this DOI exist" would pass them forever.

`verify_citations_live.py` now fetches the **registered title** from
CrossRef and compares it against the title our own source claims for that
DOI, failing on zero content-word overlap. Titles are matched leniently
(two shared content words) because stored titles often append journal and
volume text — an exact match would be all noise.

Building it surfaced a bug in itself worth recording: the first
DOI-to-title regex used `.*?` across `re.DOTALL`, so an entry with a title
but no DOI (Lehninger, Fisher 1930) let the scan run on and pair that title
with the *next* entry's DOI. It produced a map claiming `bi201284u` was
"Lehninger Principles of Biochemistry". A mis-paired map reports false
mismatches, which is worse than no check at all; the pattern is now scoped
to a single object literal with `[^{}]*?`.

## 5. Documentation bloat is now actively harmful

The repository contains **162 markdown files**, 34 at the root and 16 in
`api-server/` alone, including three separate versions of the same
document (`CODE_QUALITY_IMPROVEMENTS.md`,
`..._COMPREHENSIVE.md`, `..._FINAL.md`) and duplicate ADRs
(`ADR_0007_Python_TypeScript_Boundary_Contract.md`) that shadow the real
records in `docs/adr/`.

This is no longer only a tidiness question. The wrong `ng.3285` citation
from §2 **also survives in `LITERATURE_BACKED_INTEGRATION.md` and
`LITERATURE_BACKING_DATABASE.md`**, which were generated by copying the
source. Fixing the code did not fix them, nothing checks them, and a reader
who finds them will get the wrong citation. Duplicated documentation
becomes a second, unguarded source of truth the moment the first one is
corrected.

Recommendation: delete the duplicated ADRs and the versioned "improvement
summary" files outright, and keep documentation in the two places that have
guards pointing at them — `docs/adr/` and `Business/build-stages/`.
Awaiting a decision before deleting anything.

## 6b. The title check found two MORE fabricated DOIs on its first run

Both resolve. Both are real papers. Neither is the paper being cited:

| cited as | DOI | what CrossRef actually registers |
|---|---|---|
| Lineweaver & Burk (1934), "The determination of enzyme dissociation constants" | `10.1021/ja01349a014` | **"THE RAMAN SPECTRUM OF GERMANIUM TETRACHLORIDE"** |
| Wright (1931), "Evolution in Mendelian populations" | `10.1038/127487a0` | **"Oceanographical Expedition of the Dana, 1928-1930"** |

That brings the total to **five wrong citations in `domain-literature.ts`**,
of which four resolved cleanly and would have survived any existence-only
check indefinitely.

- **Wright (1931)** corrected to `10.1093/genetics/16.2.97`, verified via
  PubMed (PMID 17246615, PMC1201091), *Genetics* 16(2), 97-159.
- **Lineweaver & Burk (1934)**: the wrong DOI was **removed and not
  replaced**. J. Am. Chem. Soc. 1934 predates PubMed's coverage, so no
  replacement could be verified from a primary source here, and
  substituting a plausible-looking DOI is precisely the failure being
  corrected. Journal, volume and pages are given instead — enough to locate
  the paper, and honest about what was confirmed.

## 6c. The scraper was re-checking its own corrections

Immediately after the fixes, three corrected DOIs reappeared in the
checker's output. The scrape was reading the comment lines that explain
*why* a DOI was wrong, so every documented mistake became a permanent
failing check — and worse, a permanently red report trains people to ignore
it.

`discovered_dois()` now skips comment lines. A DOI named in prose is being
discussed, not asserted. Discovered count went from 18 (including five
already-removed DOIs) to **13 genuinely cited**.

`10.1038/ng.3141` was also dropped from the hardcoded `DOIS` allowlist. It
was labelled "human mutation rate" but is a *third* distinct Rahbari DOI
alongside ng.3285 and ng.3469, and nothing in the codebase cites it. An
unverified DOI sitting in a checker's own allowlist is the checker
asserting something it never checked.
